# jaspQualityControl (development version)

## Added
* Process Capability: optional Z.bench (ST) / Z.bench (LT) columns, reporting the sigma level based on the total expected defect probability across both specification limits, alongside the existing Z (ST) / Z (LT) = 3·Cpk / 3·Ppk.
* Process Capability / control charts: added "Pooled" as a within-subgroup standard deviation estimation method (in addition to R-bar and S-bar).
* DOE Analysis: optional confidence intervals for the effects and coefficients in the coefficients table ([Issue #4640](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/4640)).
* DOE Analysis: the ANOVA table splits the Error row into Lack of fit (with F-test) and Pure error when the design has replicated runs, e.g. center points ([Issue #4640](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/4640)).
* DOE Analysis, response optimizer: optional table with the predicted responses at the optimal solution (and at manually set factor levels), with standard error, confidence interval for the mean response and prediction interval for a single new run ([Issue #4640](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/4640)).

## Changed
* Control charts: the tests for special causes are now numbered as in Nelson's paper, AIAG and Minitab. Test 4 is oscillation (14 points alternating), Test 5 is 2 out of 3 points beyond 2 sigma on the same side, Test 6 is 4 out of 5 points beyond 1 sigma on the same side, Test 7 is 15 points within 1 sigma and Test 8 is 8 points beyond 1 sigma on either side. Before, Tests 4 to 8 were 2 out of 3, 15 within, 8 beyond, 4 out of 5 and oscillation, and the two same-side tests were called "increasing variation". Saved analyses are upgraded and keep the tests they used; only the numbers and names in the options, the test tables and the help changed ([Issue #4671](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/4671)).
* DOE Analysis: the contour and surface plots now come with a note that states which slice of the model they show: continuous predictors that are not on the axes of a plot are held at their mean, and the prediction is averaged over the levels of the discrete factors and blocks ([INTERNAL-jasp#3395](https://github.com/jasp-stats/INTERNAL-jasp/issues/3395)).
* DOE Analysis: the note on aliased terms that were removed from the model now says that their effects are assumed to be zero, and names the terms each of them is aliased with ([INTERNAL-jasp#3396](https://github.com/jasp-stats/INTERNAL-jasp/issues/3396)).
* DOE Analysis, uncoded model: the footnote of the coefficients table now says that the level of a two-level discrete factor shown in the Discrete Predictor Levels table has contrast +1 and the other level −1, so the direction of its effect is clear ([PR #652](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jaspQualityControl/pull/652)).
* DOE Analysis, Factorial Design and Response Surface Design: help moved from markdown files into the QML `info` properties.
* The Process Capability Study help now explains how to read changes from baseline when a stage label recurs. For a sequence A, B, A, both later stages are compared with the first A period ([PR #639](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jaspQualityControl/pull/639)).
* DOE Analysis: the contour plot is now drawn with ggplot2 instead of base graphics, so it renders correctly in interactive (plotly) mode, and always shows its legend. The "Show legend next to graph" option was removed; surface plots no longer show a color legend.

## Fixed
* Test-retest: saved studies with a missing or invalid selected process standard deviation or tolerance now request a positive value and leave the affected percentage blank, while retaining the gauge estimate and any valid percentage. Previously an invalid value could silently become 1. Already-saved positive values cannot reveal whether an earlier upgrade supplied them.
* DOE Factorial Design: blocks are now confounded with higher-order interactions (e.g. ABCDE for 5 factors in 2 blocks) instead of with 2-factor interactions that could be kept clear (e.g. AE). Only when a 2-factor interaction cannot be avoided (e.g. 4 factors in 4 blocks), or the other effects of the design would be more aliased (e.g. a lower resolution), are blocks confounded with 2-factor interactions; FrF2's search can also miss a clear assignment (e.g. 9 factors in 128 runs with 8 blocks keep CG confounded with blocks), which the Alias Structure then shows. Designs with 8 or more factors in 64 runs with 8 blocks, with more than 12 factors in 4 or 8 blocks, or with more than 20 factors keep the previous block assignment, as finding the better one takes FrF2 too long. The Alias Structure table now has a "Blocks = ..." row that names the effects confounded with the blocks, and no longer says that there is no aliasing among the main effects and 2-factor interactions when blocks are confounded with them. Earlier worksheets and R syntax retain their block assignments. The Blocks menu identifies legacy assignments and lets users switch to the current assignment. The alias table includes a factor-code key when worksheet names differ. ([INTERNAL-jasp#3432](https://github.com/jasp-stats/INTERNAL-jasp/issues/3432)).
* Process Capability Study: the process summary tables showed LSL, Target and USL rounded to the set number of decimals, so that small limits (e.g. 0.0001) read 0; they now show the entered limits. The x-axis labels of the probability plot and of the capability histogram with "Compress x-axis" no longer read 0 for data in small units either ([Issue #4700](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/4700)).
* Process Capability Study reports (normal and non-normal): the process summary now shows an asterisk for an unspecified target or specification limit, where it showed the hidden default value (e.g. Target 0, or LSL -1 when only a USL was set); a 0 that was entered is still shown as 0 ([PR #656](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jaspQualityControl/pull/656)).
* Type 1 Instrument Capability: in the run chart, the line labels ("Mean", "Ref.", "Mean + 3s", "Ref. + 0.10 * tol.", ...) were partly cut off at the edges of the plot and the "Mean" and "Ref." labels could cover each other; the Mean ± ks lines and their labels were left out when they lay beyond the data, the reference value and the tolerance lines. All lines and labels are now shown in full inside the plot, and the x-axis title "Observation" is translated.
* Variables Charts for Subgroups, Process Capability Study: in the R and s charts a subgroup with one observation is now treated like an empty subgroup (no point, the centre line and limits of the previous subgroup); before, the R chart had no centre line or limits there, the s chart centre line dropped to 0, and the labels could read NA, NaN or `CL = 0` ([Issue #4697](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/4697)).
* DOE Factorial Design: Blocks and Centre points that the design type does not use no longer change the Design Summary and the design. Split-plot designs had kept the values set before switching (e.g. Total runs 12 and 2 blocks for an 8-run design, factor levels 1/2 instead of -1/1); designs with a specified generator counted blocks that FrF2 did not create. Both now have one block, split-plot designs no centre points, and the Blocks setting is disabled for designs with a specified generator ([Issue #4695](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/4695)).
* DOE Factorial Worksheet: factor names that match worksheet metadata, such as Replication, no longer replace treatment values or metadata in the table and export.
* DOE Response Surface Design: with discrete factors and a random run order, the runs were not randomised across the discrete levels: all runs of the first level came first, followed by the same order for the next level. The run order is now random over the whole design, so the discrete factor levels are mixed ([INTERNAL-jasp#3437](https://github.com/jasp-stats/INTERNAL-jasp/issues/3437)).
* DOE Response Surface Design, Box-Behnken with 4 or 5 continuous factors (blocked designs): with a random run order, the runs of all blocks were mixed in one random order, so the design could not be run block by block. The runs are now randomised within each block and the blocks follow each other (also with discrete factors and replicates).
* DOE Create Factorial Worksheet: the Standard Order of blocked designs with centre points gave the centre points of a block the same number and did not start the factorial runs at 1 (e.g. 1, 1, 2, 2, 3 to 10 for 8 runs in 2 blocks with 2 centre points per block). Every run now has its own number: the factorial runs keep their number from the design without centre points and the centre points follow them, block by block (9 to 12).
* Process Capability Study: when the non-normal distribution cannot be fitted, the Process summary shows the error without the "Error : " prefix. A historical threshold of the 3-parameter Weibull or log-normal distribution at or above the smallest observation now gives an error that says the threshold must be smaller than the smallest observation, instead of blaming the distribution ([Issue #4691](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/4691)).
* Process Capability Study: with "Show Report" on, a non-normal distribution that could not be fitted ended the whole analysis ("This analysis terminated unexpectedly"), and so did a probability plot that could not be drawn. The report now shows the error in place of the capability plot, the probability plot or the capability tables, and keeps its other parts.
* Process Capability Study, non-normal capability (Weibull, log-normal, gamma, log-logistic): the X-bar/R, X-bar/s, X-bar/mR and X-mR control charts (also in the report) showed zeros as the replacement value used for the fit (half the smallest positive value); they now show the data as entered, for every distribution ([Issue #4690](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/4690)).
* Attribute Agreement Analysis: with ratings or a standard of more than two categories, a positive reference no longer replaces the agreement tables and plots by the error "Invalid reference and/or results were inserted.". Only the study effectiveness summary now shows an error, which says that the miss rate and false alarm rate need exactly two categories ([Issue #4699](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/4699)).
* Gauge r&R (non-replicable): the range and average charts by operator (and the measurements by part x operator plot) were not updated when the Operator or Part variable or the data format changed, and charts ticked before the variables were assigned stayed empty. After a change to an unbalanced design, the measurements by operator plot no longer shows the previous variables, and the unbalanced-design error goes away once the design is balanced again. The report no longer stops with an error when "Graph variation components" is unticked, and the measurements by part plot is shown (empty) before the variables are assigned, like the other plots ([INTERNAL-jasp#3434](https://github.com/jasp-stats/INTERNAL-jasp/issues/3434)).
* Gauge r&R (non-replicable): ticking "Traffic light chart" before the measurement, operator and part variables were all assigned (or removing one of them afterwards) stopped the analysis with "object 'studyVar' not found"; the chart is now shown once all variables are assigned, as in Gauge r&R (crossed).
* Process Capability Study: the X-mR chart in the report ignored the stages and the specification limits (it showed one set of limits for all stages, no specification lines, and missed some flagged points); it now matches the X-mR control chart. With a moving range length above 2, the limits of the individuals chart now use that length as well, in the control chart and in the report; if the data or a stage have no complete moving range of that length, both charts show the error (before, the individuals chart was shown alone), as in Variables Charts for Individuals ([INTERNAL-jasp#3431](https://github.com/jasp-stats/INTERNAL-jasp/issues/3431)).
* DOE Response Surface Design, Box-Behnken: the Replicates setting was ignored in the design and the export, although the Design Summary counted the replicated runs. The whole design (incl. centre points) is now repeated for each replicate, with a Replication column; blocked designs keep their own blocks in each replicate and unblocked designs have one block, as counted in "Total blocks" ([INTERNAL-jasp#3433](https://github.com/jasp-stats/INTERNAL-jasp/issues/3433)).
* Control charts: the CL, LCL and UCL labels (and the LSL, target and USL labels of the control charts and of the capability histogram in Process Capability Study) no longer read 0 for data in small units; they now show 3 significant digits when the set number of decimals would leave fewer than 2 ([Issue #4681](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/4681)).
* Process Capability Study: with the Johnson or Yeo-Johnson (auto) transformation, the capability plot did not draw the overlaid normal distribution; it now shows the curve based on the total standard deviation of the transformed data ([INTERNAL-jasp#3195](https://github.com/jasp-stats/INTERNAL-jasp/issues/3195)).
* DOE Analysis: the VIF column of the coefficients table now shows the variance inflation factor of each estimated coefficient (1 / (1 - R²) of its column on the other estimated columns), also when some coefficients are aliased (for the coefficients that have a unique estimate), where it used to be empty. Before, a factor with more than two levels showed the generalized VIF of the whole factor on each of its coefficients ([INTERNAL-jasp#3390](https://github.com/jasp-stats/INTERNAL-jasp/issues/3390), [INTERNAL-jasp#3355](https://github.com/jasp-stats/INTERNAL-jasp/issues/3355)).
* DoE Analysis: the note on removed aliased terms ("aliased with ...") no longer leaves out a covariate in large units; the aliased terms named no longer depend on the units of the covariates ([Issue #4692](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/4692)).
* Process Capability: the Anderson-Darling p-value in the probability table of the Weibull distribution now uses the null distribution of the statistic for a Weibull fit with estimated parameters; before, it used the approximation for the normal distribution, which gave p-values that were too small, especially for small samples. In the table footnote, "AD" is no longer partly in italics ([Issue #4682](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/4682), [Issue #4683](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/4683)).
* Control charts with missing observations: the X-bar, R and s charts (Variables Charts for Subgroups, Process Capability Study) showed infinite or missing limits and labels such as `UCL = Inf` for rows without observations, and the cumulative sum chart drew its limits at 0 when the first observation is missing. Empty rows now keep the limits of the previous row, and a missing first observation has no limit. The autocorrelation plot of Variables Charts for Individuals is now also shown when the X-mR chart shows an error ([Issue #4673](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/4673), [Issue #4674](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/4674), [Issue #4675](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/4675)).
* Gauge Linearity and Bias: the bias and linearity graph no longer cuts off data points, the zero line or the confidence band of the regression line when the largest bias is negative, zero or only slightly positive (the y-axis ended below them, so the red line was refitted to the remaining points), and now draws the zero line when all biases are positive ([INTERNAL-jasp#3435](https://github.com/jasp-stats/INTERNAL-jasp/issues/3435)).
* Gauge Linearity and Bias: with a single reference value (one part), the Regression model table showed the standard error of the intercept as the slope, with the other columns shifted, and the regression equation, %Linearity and percent process variation graph used that number. The Regression model table now shows the error "Linearity requires at least 2 different reference values.", the regression equation and gauge linearity tables and the regression line are left out, and the bias table and the biases in the graphs are still shown.
* Test-retest (Range method): the scatter plot of the 1st vs 2nd measurement no longer drops points and refits the line when there are negative measurements; the axes now always contain all points, and with "Display all measurements" also all jittered copies ([INTERNAL-jasp#3436](https://github.com/jasp-stats/INTERNAL-jasp/issues/3436)).
* Gauge r&R and Gauge r&R (non-replicable): in the report, the labels of the Components of variation plot overlapped. The report now shows the abbreviated labels "Repeat" and "Reprod" ([INTERNAL-jasp#3382](https://github.com/jasp-stats/INTERNAL-jasp/issues/3382)).
* Gauge r&R (non-replicable): data from only one operator stopped the analysis with "contrasts can be applied only to factors with 2 or more levels"; it now shows the error "At least 2 operators are required.", like the unbalanced-design errors.
* DOE Factorial Design: with replications, the Block column of the design table showed the replication number, so a design with 1 block and 3 replications had blocks 1 to 3. The replications now have their own Replication column and without blocking the Block column is 1; with blocking each replicate keeps its own blocks, as counted in "Total blocks" ([Issue #2976](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/2976)).
* Attribute Agreement Analysis: Kendall's tau now works for ordinal ratings and standards given as text (e.g. poor < fair < good), using the order of their levels, which a footnote shows; the ratings and standard lists now accept ordinal columns. A text standard that is not ordinal now gives an error, as does an ordinal text standard whose levels are in another order than those of ordinal text ratings; before, Kendall's tau used the order of the standard's labels without notice ([Issue #4590](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/4590)).
* DOE Analysis: the response optimizer no longer reports a composite desirability of 0 when acceptable settings exist between the design points. When no design point reaches the bounds, it now first moves towards the acceptable region and then maximizes the desirability. The optimum is also polished with a derivative-free search, because the desirability has kinks (for example where a prediction equals its target) at which the first search could stop short of the optimum. The note shown with a desirability of 0 now says that no acceptable setting was found and that very narrow feasible regions may be missed by the numerical search ([INTERNAL-jasp#3388](https://github.com/jasp-stats/INTERNAL-jasp/issues/3388), [INTERNAL-jasp#3389](https://github.com/jasp-stats/INTERNAL-jasp/issues/3389)).
* DOE Analysis: ANOVA, effect plots and AIC selection use coded units independently of the coefficient display. Non-hierarchical or inconsistent fits now give an error, and the old squared-term coding option is retired. The final stepwise model and actual factor coding are reported ([Issue #4686](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/4686)).
* DOE Analysis: Lenth screening is restricted to orthogonal two-level effects with equal precision; effect-plot titles state the fixed .05 level. The response optimizer excludes failed runs and reports the best solution found. Concise notes identify alias assumptions, projected replication, curvature limitations, plot settings and search bounds.
* Process Capability: the non-normal capability analysis replaced every exact zero by half the smallest positive value for all distributions, so the fit, Pp/Ppk, the expected ppm and the sample mean and standard deviation of the process summary described altered data. Recorded zeros are now kept for every capability distribution. Two-parameter Weibull, log-normal, gamma and log-logistic fits report that positive measurements are required. Changing the capability distribution no longer changes the Anderson–Darling result for a fixed diagnostic distribution. The 3-parameter Weibull fit now also works when the data contain a zero. A 3-parameter Weibull or log-normal fit whose threshold, also a historical one, is at or above the smallest observation now stops with the estimation error instead of returning the parameters of another fit, except for the valid shifted-exponential Weibull case with historical shape 1 and a historical threshold equal to the minimum. A log-normal probability plot distribution that cannot be fitted to the data shows an error in the probability table instead of stopping the analysis ([INTERNAL-jasp#3417](https://github.com/jasp-stats/INTERNAL-jasp/issues/3417)).
* Functions of jaspBase (`gsubInteractionSymbol`, `.quitAnalysis`, `mathExpression`, `createJaspColumn`, `%setOrRetrieve%`) were called without being imported, so they were only found when jaspBase was attached, not in the engine of the JASP development build. There, every DOE Analysis run with a response and factors stopped with "could not find function", and so did every Process Capability run with a data transformation. When the Attribute Agreement design check or the Multivariate Control Charts stage check found a problem, or when the T² column was exported, the error read "could not find function" instead of its message. The functions are now imported ([INTERNAL-jasp#3394](https://github.com/jasp-stats/INTERNAL-jasp/issues/3394)).
* Variables Charts for Subgroups, Process Capability and Time Weighted Charts: with long format data, a manual subgroup size and a Stages variable with empty cells, the observations without a stage were removed before the data were cut into subgroups, so all later subgroups shifted and mixed observations of neighbouring subgroups (and stages), and the plot note falsely said that subgroups span more than one stage. Subgroups are now formed from the original row positions, each subgroup takes its first non-missing stage, and the observations without a stage are left out of their own subgroup only.
* DOE Analysis: with "Use alias names", each factor now keeps the same letter (in the order of the factor lists, discrete factors first), whatever the model terms; before, the equation, coefficients table and Pareto and normal effect plots could label effects with another factor's letter when both factor lists were used or the model was reduced. A factor named by a single letter keeps it, no factor gets the name of another column as its letter, and the tables and effect plots show an alias legend.
* Process Capability: missing timestamps and partially observed subgroups no longer suppress plots when every stage has measurements. Histograms and probability plots with an odd number of stages now support interactive display.
* Control charts and Process Capability with stages: a recurring stage value (e.g., A, B, A) now forms separate stages in row order, each with its own limits and estimates, and moving ranges no longer span another stage; before, all rows with the same value were pooled into one stage and plotted out of time order. Gauge R&R charts by operator are unchanged.
* Variables Charts for Subgroups and Process Capability Study control charts: a stage whose rows are all missing no longer gets limit labels that read NaN/NA; it shows no limit lines or labels, and a note below the chart names it.
* Gauge r&R: the traffic light chart now updates when "Alpha interaction removal" changes; it kept the %Study variation / %Tolerance of the previous model (with or without the Part x Operator interaction).
* Test-retest (Range method): the traffic light chart no longer shows a %GRR of process variation when Process std. dev. is unticked; it used the hidden field value (1 by default, or a value typed before unticking), so an unacceptable gauge could look green. The chart now shows a panel only for the ticked reference value (process std. dev. and/or tolerance) and asks for one when neither is ticked.
* Process Capability: the Box-Cox transformation (manual and auto λ) now stops with an error when a measurement plus the shift is zero or negative. Previously such values were silently dropped, so the sample size, capability indices and expected and observed ppm came from the remaining data, and the observed ppm missed exactly the parts furthest outside the specification.
* Control charts for attributes: the p, u, Laney p' and Laney u' charts now colour a point red when it fails any test listed in the test table (test 1 beyond limit, test 2 shift or test 3 trend), as the np and c charts do. Previously only points beyond the limits were red, so shifts and trends inside the limits were not marked in the plot, nor anywhere with "Show Report". The individuals chart of the X-mR chart now marks points failing any of the six tests in its table, not only tests 1-3.
* Gauge r&R: the range and average charts by operator (and their test tables) are now updated when the operator, part or measurement variables or the data format change, and are removed when Type 3 study is ticked; previously they kept the old grouping, limits and test violations.
* Process Capability: editing the subgroup size (or the data format) no longer resets a chosen X-bar & R or X-bar & mR control chart to X-bar & s, which also switched the within std. dev. method from R-bar to S-bar and changed the within std. dev. and Cp/Cpk. The chart type and the std. dev. method now only change automatically when the subgroup size changes to or from 1.
* Variables charts for subgroups: with data format "Across rows", adding, removing or swapping Measurements columns now updates the control chart, its limits and the test tables. Previously the output for the previously selected columns was kept.
* Factorial Design: without "Set seed", the random run order is now drawn from a seed that is saved with the analysis, so it stays the same after the .jasp file is reopened. Previously the seed was kept only for the session (or with "Store analysis state" on), so after reopening the first rerun (e.g., switching to coded units or exporting) silently gave every run a new position. The seed used is shown below the design table. Designs saved before this fix without "Set seed" get a new run order once.
* DOE Analysis: a run with a missing value of one response is now only left out of the analysis of that response. Previously it was left out of the ANOVA, coefficients, model summary, plots and response optimizer of every response, without a note. The Model Summary table now has a footnote with the number of runs left out because of a missing response, predictor, block or covariate. A response that misses every run of one level of a discrete factor or block cannot be analysed: it now shows an error in its own output (and in the response optimizer, if selected), while the other responses are still analysed.
* DOE residuals-versus-order plots now show the original worksheet run numbers when a predictor or response is missing. The same run numbers appear in the four-in-one plot ([PR #653](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jaspQualityControl/pull/653)).
* Control charts for attributes: the y-axis of the np and c charts no longer starts above the lower control limit when that limit is well above 0, and the individuals and moving range chart for attributes now covers all data points. Previously the LCL line and points below it (including out-of-control points) or beyond the limits of the X-mR chart could be missing from the plot.
* Type 2 and 3 Gauge r&R: the "Type of model for F-statistic" now defaults to Random effects, as in AIAG and Minitab; it defaulted to Fixed effects, so the two-way ANOVA table with interaction tested Part and Operator against repeatability instead of the Part x Operator interaction (e.g. Operator F 34.44 instead of 79.41 for the AIAG example). Saved analyses keep their setting.
* Gauge r&R, Gauge r&R (non-replicable) and Test-retest: the traffic light chart printed every value above 100% as "100.00%", while the tables showed the real value (e.g. a %Tolerance of 119.22%). The chart now prints the real value; only the marker stays at the end of the axis. In Test-retest, a process standard deviation or tolerance of 0 now leaves that percentage blank with a footnote instead of showing ∞.
* Process Capability: Box-Cox (auto) with the Log-Lik or SD method now estimates λ from the variation within subgroups. Previously it grouped the values by their position within the subgroup (the measurement column), so the variation between subgroups entered the estimate and λ, the transformed specification limits, all capability indices and expected ppm were off and changed when values were reordered within a subgroup. With subgroup size 1 the overall variation is used.
* Attribute Agreement Analysis: the miss rate and false alarm rate of the study effectiveness summary were computed as if the positive reference were the accept (good) category. With the defect category as positive reference (e.g. "No-Go", or 1 in a 0 = OK / 1 = defect coding) the two rates and their verdicts were swapped. A new option "Positive reference is the" (accept (good) category, the default, or reject (defect) category) now sets which category is good, and a footnote names the accept category used.
* Variables charts for individuals: changing the Measurement variable now refreshes the X-mR chart, its limits and test tables, and the report. Previously the previous variable's chart and tables stayed in the output. The report now also updates when the autocorrelation confidence level changes and is removed when Report is unchecked.
* Gauge Linearity: the p-value of the Total row in the Gauge bias table now tests the average bias against the pooled within-part standard deviation (repeatability), as the per-part rows, AIAG and Minitab do. Previously it used the residual standard deviation of the bias regression, which also contains any lack of fit (e.g. curvature), so a clear average bias could be reported as non-significant.
* Process Capability: the confidence bands of the Weibull probability plot (also in the report) are now computed from the closed-form observed information instead of a finite-difference Hessian. For data that vary little relative to their location (Weibull shape of about 100 or more) the finite-difference bands were off, by about 2% of the half-width at shape 280 and 30% at shape 930 in an example. For data that vary even less (shape of about 5000 or more, e.g. a standard deviation of 0.03 at a mean of 100) the bands were usually missing.
* Control charts for attributes: with unequal subgroup sizes, the out-of-control test table of the u, Laney p' and Laney u' charts checked test 1 (beyond limit) against the first subgroup's limits only, so it could list points that the plot shows within their limits and miss points beyond them. Test 1 now uses each subgroup's own limits. The p chart table never reported test 2 (shift) or test 3 (trend), with equal or unequal subgroup sizes, because each subgroup was tested on its own; these tests are now evaluated on the whole series of plotted proportions, as for the other charts. The tables of the np, c, u and Laney charts with constant limits and of the individuals and moving range charts are unchanged.
* Process Capability: Box-Cox transformation with λ < 0 and no continuity adjustment now uses y = −(x + shift)^λ, so the lower and upper specification limits keep their roles. Previously the transformed limits were swapped, which stopped the analysis (both limits) or gave capability indices and ppm for the wrong tail (one limit). Results now equal those with the continuity adjustment.
* Process Capability: the non-normal capability analysis with the logistic distribution now accepts negative measurements; previously any negative value stopped the analysis with "Dataset contains negative numbers", although the logistic distribution covers all real values.
* Control charts for attributes: the p chart no longer shows a negative lower control limit. When the subgroup sizes differed by at most 25%, it drew constant limits from the average subgroup size, centred on the unweighted mean of the subgroup proportions instead of the centre line and not bounded to [0, 1], while the out-of-control test used the limits of each subgroup, so the plot and the test could disagree. The limits are now always computed for each subgroup's size around the pooled proportion and bounded to [0, 1], as in Minitab; with unequal sizes they are drawn as steps. The upper control limit of the Laney p' chart is capped at 1 ([Issue #3225](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/3225)).
* DOE Analysis: for saturated models (no error degrees of freedom) of two-level factorial terms, the Pareto chart and normal plot show the effects with Lenth's pseudo standard error and margin of error instead of failing; other saturated models (e.g. with squared terms) and plots without effects give an error ([Issue #3861](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/3861), [Issue #4158](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/4158)). The normal plot of effects now ranks the effects by their value (some points had the percentile of another effect). Without alias names, the effect plots no longer squeeze the chart to the right (term labels are decoded before the layout is made), and the reference line of the normal plot no longer runs along the top edge for large effects.
* Probability of detection: the confidence band of the detection plot used the one-sided critical value, so a band labelled 95% was a 90% interval. It is now a two-sided interval at the chosen level (computed on the link scale and back-transformed) and therefore wider; the fitted curve and all tables are unchanged.
* Process Capability: with stages, the probability plot (also in the report) now fits the line and confidence bands to each stage's data; previously every stage showed the fit of the last stage.
* Process Capability: the 95% confidence bands of the Weibull probability plot (also in the report) now use the standard normal critical value (1.96); previously they used 1.31 and covered only about 80%.
* Process Capability: the confidence bands of the probability plot no longer disappear for data that vary on the order of 0.001 (e.g. measurements in small units); the numerical derivatives now use steps relative to small fitted parameters.
* Process Capability: the Anderson-Darling p-value in the probability table now always follows D'Agostino & Stephens (1986) for the modified statistic. For a statistic just below 0.6 whose modified value is at least 0.6 (small samples), it previously reported the much larger p-value for known parameters; for very large statistics it no longer becomes larger than 1, infinite or NaN.
* Process Capability: the confidence intervals for Cp and Cpk in the process capability (within) table used the wrong degrees of freedom (subgroup size and number of subgroups were swapped, and the within std. dev. method was ignored), so the intervals were too narrow in the usual case of more subgroups than observations per subgroup. The degrees of freedom now follow the method used to estimate the within std. dev.: pooled Σ(nᵢ − 1), R-bar 0.9·Σ(nᵢ − 1), S-bar f(n̄)·Σ(nᵢ − 1), and the number of complete moving ranges for individuals. With a historical std. dev., these intervals are no longer shown.
* Control charts for attributes: the Laney p' and u' charts standardized every subgroup with the first subgroup's size, so with unequal subgroup sizes the control limits were wrong and depended on which subgroup came first. Each subgroup is now standardized with its own size (Laney, 2002); results with equal subgroup sizes are unchanged.
* Time weighted charts: singleton subgroups across multiple measurement columns now use moving ranges in row order, preserving entirely missing rows as gaps.
* Process Capability: missing moving ranges no longer discard the capability tables or the report. A control chart that cannot be drawn is left out with a note below the plot (or the plot shows the error if neither chart can be drawn); on the report, it is replaced by its error. Unavailable within standard deviations remain missing while overall estimates are retained.
* Time weighted charts, variables charts for individuals: when the std. dev. is estimated from moving ranges and a stage (or the data) has no complete moving range (fewer non-missing observations than the moving range length, or every range interrupted by a missing value), the chart shows an error naming the stage instead of stopping the analysis or drawing NaN limits. In the reports, only the affected chart is replaced by its error.
* Time weighted charts: the EWMA chart estimates the std. dev. of individual observations with missing values from moving ranges, like the CUSUM chart (previously it stopped with an error, or, with a grouping variable or across-rows data, drew zero-width control limits).
* Time weighted charts: after a subgroup without observations, the CUSUM and EWMA charts continue from the last observed subgroup with finite control limits (previously both lines stopped at the gap and the EWMA limits became infinite).
* Time weighted charts: notes about removed observations (not assigned to a stage or subgroup) are shown below the charts.
* Time weighted charts: the chart tables no longer stay in the output when the report is shown.
* Time weighted charts: the CUSUM moving range length field is shown based on the CUSUM (not the EWMA) std. dev. source.
* DOE Analysis: with "Display results in coded units", discrete factors with more than two levels were coded as one numeric column with evenly spaced values from -1 to +1 (1 df instead of k-1), which gave wrong ANOVA, lack-of-fit test, S, R², p-values and effects. They now stay factors (sum-to-zero contrasts), as in uncoded units, and the manual low/high table shows "Not available" for them and for blocks (whose low/high levels were already ignored). Levels without runs are named below the coefficients table. Also affected the response surface ANOVA with "Use coded data to calculate squared terms" and stepwise selection on coded data ([Issue #4638](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/4638)).
* DOE Analysis: aliasing removed whole terms when only some of their contrasts were aliased, e.g. discrete factors with levels -1/0/1 where level 0 only occurs at the center points (the model reduced to one factor), or an interaction A ✻ F where A is constant within one level of F. Only the aliased contrasts are now left out; the term keeps its estimable contrasts (with fewer df) and a footnote names it, plus a hint to enter factors with a shared center level as continuous predictors. Type III sums of squares are not defined for such models, so with Type III selected the ANOVA table then shows Type II sums of squares, with a footnote saying so ([INTERNAL-jasp#3393](https://github.com/jasp-stats/INTERNAL-jasp/issues/3393)). Coefficients and effects that depend on which aliased contrasts are left out (e.g., on the order of the factor levels) are left empty, and the regression equation is then not shown. The response optimizer, prediction tables and optimization plot only use settings whose response the data can estimate (e.g., not combinations of factor levels without runs), and show an error when they find none (e.g., a factor held constant within blocks); contour/surface plots without a unique prediction, and Pareto and normal effect plots without a unique effect, also show an error. Alias footnotes in coded units now come from the coded model, and VIF gets a footnote when it is not available.
* DOE Analysis, factorial designs: with "Display results in coded units" off, the ANOVA treated every continuous predictor as a factor. Center points made all but one term look aliased (e.g. only the first factor left in the ANOVA), and a predictor with many distinct values gave the error "residual df = 0". The ANOVA now uses the predictors in coded units, so it is the same in coded and uncoded units; a continuous predictor with 3 levels has 1 df instead of 2.
* DOE Analysis: a model without error degrees of freedom shows sequential sums of squares; the ANOVA table now labels them Type I instead of the selected type and says why.
* DOE Analysis: the VIF column of the coefficients table showed the VIF of a main effect for interaction terms and nothing for squared terms; each coefficient now shows the (generalized) VIF of its own term.
* DOE Analysis: with type II/III sums of squares, the ANOVA table's Model and Total rows no longer just add up the individual term SS (which is only valid for type I). Model SS is now SST − SSE, with F/p from the overall regression test, and Total SS is SST. The grouped rows (Linear, Squared, Interaction terms, Covariates) are now tested jointly and report MS, F and p.
* Test–retest: limit across-rows input to two measurement columns, reject extra single-column measurement sets (previously the third set was silently ignored) and report duplicate or unmatched readings; rows with missing values are still excluded.
* Attribute agreement: with more than one trial, Kendall's tau stopped with an error (with a standard) or showed extra rows (without a standard). Appraiser vs standard is now Kendall's tau-b of each trial with the standard, averaged over trials (as in Minitab); between appraisers it is Kendall's tau-b over all combinations of the two appraisers' ratings of each part, so it does not depend on the trial order. Results with one trial are unchanged. Without a standard, the empty standard column is no longer shown ([Issue #4677](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/4677)).
* Attribute agreement: return valid confidence-interval endpoints for zero or perfect agreement.
* Attribute agreement: in wide format, "Between appraisers", "All appraisers vs standard", Fleiss' kappa between appraisers, the Cohen's kappa correlations and, with a single trial, Kendall's tau compared the appraisers' ratings by row position, so data in run order (rows not sorted by part within every appraiser) gave wrong agreement. Ratings are now matched by part. In both formats, a design in which an appraiser does not rate every part, or rates a part more often than another, and a part whose reference differs between rows, now give an error instead of wrong results or a crash ([INTERNAL-jasp#2812](https://github.com/jasp-stats/INTERNAL-jasp/issues/2812)).
* Attribute agreement: with more than one trial, the correlations between appraisers paired trial k of one appraiser with trial k of another, so they changed with the trial order (e.g. after reordering the rows of long-format data). They now pair all ratings of the same part; results with one trial are unchanged. The table, previously titled "Cohen's kappa correlations summary", is renamed "Correlations between appraisers' ratings", as it shows Pearson correlations of the ratings, not kappas. Appraiser names that are numbers or contain spaces or punctuation (e.g. `1`, `B L`) now show their values instead of empty columns, and a correlation of exactly 1 is shown instead of "-". In long format with more than one trial, a footnote to Fleiss' kappa vs standard (averaged over trials, as in Minitab) says that trial k is the k-th row of an appraiser and part ([Issue #4678](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/4678)).
* Probability of detection: identify natural-log coordinates in covariate labels, reference tables and explanatory footnotes.
* Attribute charts: allow defect rates above one and reject defective-item counts exceeding the sample size.
* Process Capability: observed nonconformance rates and changes from baseline now use each stage's valid measurement count when stage sizes differ.
* Attribute charts: label u and Laney u axes as defects per unit instead of proportions.
* Multivariate control charts: keep exported T² values aligned with their original rows when incomplete observations are omitted.
* Multivariate control charts: fix Phase II errors and missing observation labels caused by automatic naming.
* Control charts and process capability: prevent overflow when calculating bias-corrected standard deviations.
* Control charts with stages: the test results tables now report the chart position and timestamp of out-of-control points in every stage after the first, instead of their position within the stage ([Issue #4672](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/4672)).
* Variables charts, rare event charts (custom tests), and the control charts in Process Capability Studies and Gauge r&R: Test 3 (trend) and Test 4 (oscillation) now flag the point that completes the set number of points, e.g. the 6th point of a trend with Nelson laws and the 7th with JASP Default; previously one more point was needed ([PR #642](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jaspQualityControl/pull/642)).
* Control charts with interleaved stages (stages alternating between rows): x-axis labels and test results timestamps now belong to the plotted subgroup in "Across rows" data and in time weighted charts with timestamps.
* Cumulative sum charts with stages: the names of the stages after the first are now shown halfway along their stage instead of at its last point.
* Control charts, time weighted charts and process capability: the unbiasing constants d2 and d3 for subgroup sizes or moving range lengths above 50 are now computed exactly instead of by a polynomial approximation that was wrong beyond 100 (e.g. d2 = 5.64 instead of 14.3 for n = 250), so R-bar and moving range based std. dev. and control limits are correct for large subgroups.
* Time weighted charts: the exponentially weighted moving average chart now offers S-bar and R-bar as std. dev. estimation method for data in wide format ("Across rows"), instead of only X-mR, which stopped the analysis with two or more measurement columns.
* Rare event charts: the interval type "Time" no longer always fails with "Date/time conversion returned no valid values", and its intervals are always converted to minutes, so the chart no longer labels intervals given in hours as minutes.
* Rare event charts: the G chart probability limits are corrected. The LCL is 0 whenever the event probability is at least 0.00135 (it was slightly above 0, so every gap of 0 was flagged by test 1), and the UCL and center line are no longer sometimes one unit too high (e.g. the center line for event probability 0.5 is 0 instead of 1). All limits are bounded below by 0, the smallest possible gap.
* On rare-event G charts, Test 9 now flags the point that completes the selected number of consecutive zeros, including when the series begins with zeros ([PR #644](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jaspQualityControl/pull/644)).
* Stepwise model selection in the Response Surface design now respects marginality: a squared term is no longer retained without its corresponding main effect.
* Process Capability: distribution/fit density curves no longer render flat in interactive (plotly) plots; the curves are now drawn with `geom_line` instead of `stat_function`.
* Gauge r&R (crossed): with "Fixed effects" as type of model for the F-statistic, the p-values for Part and Operator in the ANOVA table with interaction now use the repeatability degrees of freedom (the error term of their F-statistics) instead of the interaction degrees of freedom, which gave incorrect p-values. A Type 3 study with this setting no longer stops with an error.
* Process Capability: the x-axis of the capability plot is no longer stretched towards the default historical scale (Weibull) or log mean (log-normal) when no historical parameter is set, which squeezed the histogram and fitted density into a narrow strip for data far from zero.
* Process Capability: the x-axis of the Weibull and log-normal probability plots no longer stretches far beyond the data, which squeezed the points into a thin strip and made the tick labels overlap. The axis now follows the data, and ticks that would crowd each other are dropped.
* Attributes agreement analysis: Fleiss' kappa no longer crashes with newer `irr` (>= 0.85) when a within-appraiser input has a single measurement column; returns NaN as before.
* DOE Analysis: the Effect column of the coefficients table is now 2 × coefficient (on the −1/+1 scale) for main effects and interactions of every order. Previously 2-way and 3-way interactions were inflated (4× and 6×), effects depended on the factor level labels, and uncoded continuous predictors and manual coding beyond ±1 gave wrong values. Effects are left blank for squared terms and factors with more than two levels, and a footnote explains the definition ([PR #525](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jaspQualityControl/pull/525)).
* DOE Analysis: no longer crashes when a discrete factor is fully removed from the model due to aliasing ([PR #525](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jaspQualityControl/pull/525)).
* DOE Analysis: the response optimizer now uses the observed maximum (maximize) or minimum (minimize) as the default target when "Set all bounds manually" is on and "Set all targets manually" is off. Previously the target silently became the value of the disabled Upper/Lower field (1 or 0), which distorted the desirability. Settings without a defined desirability (maximize with the lower bound above the target, minimize with the target above the upper bound, target outside manual bounds, lower above upper) now give an error instead of a misleading optimum. A target outside bounds taken from the data gets a footnote, and a bound equal to the target no longer gives NaN. The optimizer now starts from the first run and the three design points with the highest desirability instead of only the first run, so it no longer stalls at desirability 0 when a design point already reaches the bounds. A footnote flags a solution with desirability 0; acceptable settings that lie between the design points can still be missed. The optimizer is no longer rerun when unrelated options change. Analyses with a single predictor no longer crash, the settings footnote no longer reports an entered target as estimated from data, no empty "Optimization Plot" heading remains after a settings error, "Show optimal solution" now also hides the settings table, and an optimum on the edge of the design space is no longer flagged as extrapolation ([PR #541](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jaspQualityControl/pull/541)).
* DOE Analysis, response optimizer: with blocks or covariates in the model, the fitted responses and desirabilities added every block and covariate coefficient in full. Predictions are now averaged over the blocks and covariates are held at their mean, matching the new prediction table ([Issue #4665](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/4665)).
* DOE Analysis, response optimizer: with two or more discrete factors, the optimization plot, its summary table and the solution table's fitted response gave some factor levels no contribution (e.g. the last level of the first factor), because the factor levels were merged. The optimal settings themselves were not affected ([Issue #4665](https://github.com/jasp-stats/jasp-issues/issues/4665)).
* DOE Analysis: block and discrete factor levels without runs (e.g. all their rows filtered out or with missing responses) are dropped. Such a level made the model fall back from sum-to-zero to treatment contrasts, which the effects and the response optimizer assume.
* Gauge r&R: the gauge evaluation table of the report shows the name of the operator variable instead of its internal (encoded) column name.
* Control charts: the CL, LCL and UCL labels of values of 1000 or more keep all their decimals (e.g. 1908.333 instead of 1908.33); the shortened labels could also be rounded wrong (2092.49 instead of 2092.495).
* Process Capability, stages: the Cpm confidence interval is no longer blanked for the stages after the baseline, and the change row no longer shows the difference of the interval bounds as an interval.
* Process Capability, stages: the change (vs. baseline) rows are computed from the unrounded values, so they are the rounded difference; the difference of the rounded values could be off in the last decimal.
* Process Capability: for data with a small spread (std. dev. or log std. dev. below ~0.002, e.g. 10.000 ± 0.001), the logistic fit and the log-normal fit with a historical parameter no longer fail. The 95% confidence bands of the normal and log-normal probability plots are now computed accurately for small and large spreads; previously their variance estimates could be off by ~3% (e.g. std. dev. 0.02), and the bands were missing or wrong for very large spreads (e.g. std. dev. 100000). Other logistic estimates can change in the 4th–5th significant digit.
* Process Capability, rare event (t) charts: Weibull, gamma, log-logistic and 3-parameter Weibull fits no longer fail for data in very small or large units (e.g. Weibull data in thousandths, gamma data in hundred-thousands), and their estimates no longer change with the unit. A 3-parameter Weibull fit with a shape of at most 1 gives an error instead of arbitrary estimates (the likelihood then increases towards a threshold at the smallest observation, so there is no maximum likelihood estimate). The Weibull probability plot confidence bands no longer depend on the unit either (they were off for data in large units), and the Weibull probability table uses the same fit as the plots (a failed fit shows an error on the table). The gamma fit now solves its likelihood equations directly, so it is the exact maximum likelihood estimate also for data with little variation (e.g. 10 ± 0.02), where the previous fit could be off by several percent in the shape and hence in the expected ppm. Other estimates can change in the 4th–5th significant digit (for the Weibull fit, closer to the exact maximum likelihood estimate).
* Process Capability: the histogram density line for the log-logistic distribution now uses the log-logistic fit (it used the logistic fit, which drew an essentially flat line); a failed fit is shown on the histogram instead of stopping the analysis.
* Process Capability, non-normal: Pp, PpL, PpU, Ppk and the observed and expected ppm use the specification limits as entered; they used the limits rounded to the displayed decimals (e.g. 0.0144 as 0.014), which changed the results for limits with more decimals, such as measurements in small units.
* Process Capability: with the Kaplan-Meier rank method, the probability plot (also in the report) no longer places the largest observation at p = 1, which drew it on the upper edge of the panel; it is now placed at 90% of the distance between the previous plotting position and 1, i.e. (n - 0.1) / n without ties (a tied maximum already lies below 1 and is unchanged). The y-axis of the probability plot now extends past the 0.1% and 99.9% ticks when points fall outside them (large samples), instead of leaving those points out.
